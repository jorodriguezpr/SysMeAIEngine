Greenleaf Houseplant Care Guide

Light

Most common houseplants prefer bright, indirect light. A spot one to two meters from an east- or west-facing window works well. Direct afternoon sun through glass can scorch the leaves of ferns and calatheas, while succulents and cacti tolerate it and grow best with at least four hours of direct light per day.

Watering

Water when the top two centimeters of soil feel dry to the touch. Pour slowly until water drains from the bottom of the pot, then empty the saucer after fifteen minutes so the roots never sit in standing water. In winter most plants need about half as much water as in summer. Overwatering is the most common cause of houseplant death; yellow, soft lower leaves are the usual first sign.

Soil and pots

Always use a pot with a drainage hole. A general indoor potting mix suits most foliage plants. Add one part perlite to three parts potting mix for plants that dislike wet roots, such as snake plants and ZZ plants. Cacti and succulents need a gritty cactus mix.

Repot a plant when roots grow out of the drainage hole or circle the bottom of the pot, usually every one to two years. Choose a new pot only two to five centimeters wider than the old one; a much larger pot holds too much water around the roots.

Feeding

Feed actively growing plants once a month from spring to early autumn with a balanced liquid fertilizer diluted to half the strength printed on the label. Do not fertilize in winter, when growth slows, or for six weeks after repotting.

Humidity and temperature

Tropical plants such as calatheas, ferns and orchids like humidity above fifty percent. Group plants together or place them on a tray of wet pebbles to raise humidity. Keep all plants between 15 and 27 degrees Celsius and away from heating vents, radiators and cold drafts.

Common problems

Brown, crispy leaf tips usually mean dry air or inconsistent watering. Leggy growth with long gaps between leaves means the plant needs more light. Small webs under the leaves point to spider mites: wipe the leaves with a damp cloth and isolate the plant. White cottony spots are mealybugs, which can be removed with a cotton swab dipped in rubbing alcohol.

Pets

Pothos, philodendron, peace lily and dieffenbachia are toxic to cats and dogs if chewed. Spider plants, calatheas, parlor palms and Boston ferns are considered pet-safe.
